# Classification of young stellar objects

The widely used *class*-based naming scheme for protostellar sources was introduced by @lada1987star and is based on the slop of the [[Spectral Energy Distribution|spectral energy distribution]] in mid-infrared. Consider following expression for [[Spectral Index|spectral index]] $\alpha$:

$$
\alpha = \dfrac{\mathrm{d} \log(\lambda F_{\lambda})}{\mathrm{d}\log(\lambda)}
$$
where $F_{\lambda}$ is the flux density and $\alpha$ being calculated in the wavelength range of $2.2-20\mu\mathrm{m}$. The initial three classes introduced by @lada1987star were based on following range of values of $\alpha$:

| Class | Spectral index ($\alpha$) | Evolutionary stage                                 |
| ----- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| I     | $\alpha > 0.3$            | Protostellar/circumstellar envelopes               |
| II    | $-0.3 > \alpha > -1.6$    | T Tauri stars                                      |
| III   | $\alpha < -1.6$           | Very late stage where the disk has been dissipated |

Two more *classes* were retroactively added: **Class 0** sources show a strong emission in the sub-mm, but very faint in $\lambda < 10 \mu\mathrm{m}$ and **flat** sources are self-explanatory where the slope is close to zero ($-0.3 < \alpha < 0.3$).
