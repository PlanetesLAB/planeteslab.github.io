@armitageAstrophysicsPlanetFormation2020 defines planets as large bodies around stars not massive enough to have ever derived a substantial fraction of their luminosity from nuclear fusion. This implies a limit on the maximum mass of planets to be at the deuterium burning threshold, which is approximately $13 M_{\mathrm Jup}$ (which consequently becomes the lower mass limit for [[Brown Dwarfs|brown dwarfs]]). Lower mass limit is harder to define, and much debate has happened to find an accurate definition. In the end, following three criteria were established at the 2006 IAU summit which killed Pluto's status as a full-fledged planet:
1. Orbit around a star.[^1]
2. Must have had enough self-gravity to attain a mostly spherical shape through hydrostatic equilibrium.
3. Have cleared its neighborhood.

## Clearing of the neighborhood

Or **dynamical dominance** in other words has few formal criteria (discussed below) because the it never ever truly possible for a planetary-mass object to completely clear its orbit. 

### Stern-Levinson's $\Lambda$

$\Lambda$ is the measure of a body's ability to scatter small masses out of its orbital region over a period of time equal to the age of the Universe. In other words, how many strong gravitational encounters should a small body experience with the candidate planet over some long time $\tau$. To derive this heuristic value, @sternRegardingCriteriaPlanethood2002 develop upon the work of @opikCollisionProbabilitiesPlanets1951, who gave the probability of a small body passing sufficiently close to a planet, per orbit as

$$
P = \dfrac{s^2\;U}{\pi \sin i |U_x|}
$$

where 

$$
s^2 = \underbrace{\left(\dfrac{b}{a_p}\right)^2}_{\substack{\text{encounter} \\\ \text{cross-section}}} \overbrace{\left(1+\dfrac{2GM_p}{bV_{\infty}^2}\right)}^{\substack{\text{gravitational} \\\ \text{focusing}}},
$$

denotes the square of the target radius, $b$ is the [[Impact parameter|impact parameter]], $a_p$ is the semi-major axis of the planet, $V_{\infty}$ is the relative speed between planet and planetesimal when they are far enough apart that the planet's gravity has not yet strongly bent the trajectory, $U \equiv V_{\infty}/V_p$, is the relative encounter speed expressed in the units of planet's orbital speed $V_p$, $i$ is the inclination between the planet's and planetesimal's orbital planes and $U_x$ is the radial component of the dimensionless relative velocity $U$. For a hyperbolic encounter at deflection angle $\theta$, 


$$
\Lambda = \dfrac{m^2}{a^{3/2}} \cdot k
$$

where $m$ is the mass of the clearing body, $a$ its semi-major axis, and $k$ is a function of the orbital elements of the small body
